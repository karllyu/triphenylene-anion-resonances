================================================================================
MAD-SIGMA (ROBUST SIGMA): WHAT IT IS AND WHY IT WORKS
Worked through on Automatic-RVP data, A2_root4_fit2
================================================================================

Notation used throughout:
    x            a single value (here, the 'imag' column of clustering_input.csv)
    median(x)    the middle value of the sorted list
    MAD          Median Absolute Deviation = median( |x - median(x)| )
    MAD-sigma    1.4826 x MAD          (also called "robust sigma")
    std          ordinary standard deviation
    k            the cut threshold, in units of MAD-sigma


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PROBLEM YOU ARE TRYING TO SOLVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You have 308 Pade roots. Most are the real resonance. A handful are numerical
garbage. You want a rule that says "this point is too far out, drop it."

Any such rule needs exactly two things:

    1. A CENTER  -- too far out FROM WHERE?
    2. A RULER   -- too far COMPARED TO WHAT?

That is all. MAD-sigma is simply a particular choice of those two things.
Everything else follows from that choice.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE OBVIOUS CHOICE, AND WHY IT FAILS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The textbook answer is center = MEAN, ruler = STANDARD DEVIATION.
For the 308 imag values in A2_root4_fit2:

    mean = -1.806e-02        std = 9.470e-02

Now look at where the points actually live:

    the 8 most negative     the middle of the pack      the 5 largest
      -8.149e-01              -5.970e-04                  -3.537e-05
      -6.019e-01              -5.956e-04                  -7.981e-06
      -5.914e-01              -5.908e-04  <-- median      -7.190e-06
      -5.634e-01              -5.892e-04                  -5.419e-06
      -5.154e-01              -5.821e-04                  -4.107e-06
      -5.112e-01              -5.797e-04
      -4.326e-01              -5.794e-04
      -3.574e-01

THE MEAN, -1.806e-02, IS NOT NEAR A SINGLE ONE OF THE 308 POINTS.
It sits in empty space, roughly 30x further out than where the bulk lives.

Why? Because 11 junk points at about -0.5 contribute roughly -5.0 to the sum,
and -5.0 / 308 = -1.6e-02. Those 11 points -- 3.6% of the data -- ARE
essentially the entire mean. The other 297 barely register.

The std is worse still. It is built from SQUARED deviations, so a point that is
1000x too far away contributes 1,000,000x too much.

AND HERE IS THE TRAP:
    You wanted to use the spread to identify the outliers.
    But the outliers are what set the spread.
    The bad points inflate the very ruler you were going to measure them with.

It is circular. You are trying to catch a thief using a witness the thief
bribed.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT MAD-SIGMA DOES INSTEAD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Swap both choices for RANK-BASED ones -- quantities that depend on where values
sit in the sorted order, not on how big they are.

STEP 1 -- CENTER = THE MEDIAN.

    Sort all 308 values, take the middle one:

        median = -5.900e-04

    That sits right in the thick of the data, where 99% of the points are.
    Compare with the mean at -1.806e-02.

STEP 2 -- MEASURE EVERY POINT'S DISTANCE FROM THAT MEDIAN.

        |x - median|     for all 308 points

STEP 3 -- RULER = THE MEDIAN OF THOSE DISTANCES.

    This is the MAD, the Median Absolute Deviation. Sorting the distances:

        smallest:  8.26e-07  8.26e-07  5.55e-06  6.96e-06  ...
        middle one (#154 of 308):  2.139e-04    <-- this is the MAD
        largest:   ...  5.63e-01  5.91e-01  6.01e-01  8.14e-01

        MAD = 2.139e-04

    LOOK AT WHAT JUST HAPPENED. The four largest deviations are 5.6e-01 to
    8.1e-01 -- three to four orders of magnitude bigger than the MAD. And they
    had ZERO effect on it. The MAD only asks "which distance sits in the middle
    of the sorted list?" Making a big value even bigger does not move it out of
    last place, so nothing changes.

STEP 4 -- THE 1.4826 FACTOR.

    The MAD is a perfectly good spread measure, but it is on a different scale
    than std, so "MAD = 3" does not mean what "std = 3" means. For clean
    Gaussian data, MAD comes out to about 0.6745 x sigma. Divide by that
    (equivalently multiply by 1/0.6745 = 1.4826) and you get something that
    lands on the same number std would give for clean data:

        MAD-sigma = 1.4826 x 2.139e-04 = 3.171e-04

    That is the ONLY purpose of the constant: a unit conversion, so that
    "6 sigma" keeps its familiar meaning. On genuinely clean Gaussian data,
    MAD-sigma and std agree to about three decimals (verified: 1.0022 vs
    1.0012 on a clean Gaussian sample).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE CORE CONCEPT: BREAKDOWN POINT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the one idea worth remembering.

    BREAKDOWN POINT = what fraction of your data has to be garbage before the
                      statistic itself becomes garbage.

    MEAN and STD:     0%.
        One bad point is enough. Push a single value to infinity and the mean
        goes to infinity with it. Zero protection, by construction.

    MEDIAN and MAD:   50%.
        To drag the median off the bulk you would need to add more than 154 bad
        points -- they would have to outnumber the good data. Eleven junk points
        cannot do it. Neither could a hundred.

That is the entire mechanism. MAD-SIGMA GIVES YOU A RULER THE OUTLIERS CANNOT
STRETCH, which breaks the circularity, which is what lets a single pass work
with no iteration.

(This is also why classic sigma-clipping has to loop: it uses std, so pass one's
ruler is corrupt, so you clip, re-measure, and clip again, hoping it converges.
MAD needs no loop -- it is already correct the first time.)

    The points at -1.1136e-02 and -1.0080e-02 score 33 and 30 robust sigmas but

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE CONSEQUENCE THAT SURPRISES PEOPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    6 robust sigma   = 1.903e-03   ->  window  -2.493e-03 .. +1.313e-03
    3 ordinary sigma = 2.841e-01   ->  window  -3.022e-01 .. +2.661e-01

    (actual data range: -8.1492e-01 .. -4.1067e-06)

A 6-SIGMA ROBUST CUT IS 149x TIGHTER THAN A 3-SIGMA ORDINARY CUT.

"Six sigma" sounds permissive and "three sigma" sounds strict, but the sigma is
a different LENGTH in each case. The ordinary 3-sigma window is so wide that it
spans nearly the entire data range including most of the junk -- it flags only
11 points and misses the two subtle ones. The robust 6-sigma window hugs the
actual data and catches all 13.

So k = 6 is not a loose cut. It is a cut that is tight BECAUSE the ruler is
honest.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. HOW IT TIES BACK TO THE PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nothing above changes what gets REPORTED. MAD-sigma is used purely as a
DETECTOR, not as a replacement for any statistic in the output. The flow is:

    1. MAD-sigma identifies the 13 bad rows and drops them.

    2. StandardScaler (src/clustering.py line 33) then computes its ORDINARY
       std on the survivors -- and that std is now 3.28e-04 instead of
       9.47e-02, a 289x correction, because the corruption is gone.

    3. DBSCAN gets a sensibly scaled space, so the eps sweep explores properly
       instead of collapsing after 16 iterations (the premature break at
       src/clustering.py line 119).

    4. The imag_coeff_of_var written to output.dat is still the ordinary
       std/mean it always was -- but computed on a clean cluster, so it finally
       comes in under the 6.5% gate.

MAD-sigma appears once, does one job, and leaves no trace in the reported
physics.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THE RULE IN CODE FORM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    med = np.median(x)
    mad_sigma = 1.4826 * np.median(np.abs(x - med))
    outlier = np.abs(x - med) > k * mad_sigma        # k = 6

Applied to the 'real' and 'imag' columns only (the two DBSCAN actually
clusters on), union across the two, single pass, with a guard to skip the
column if mad_sigma == 0.
