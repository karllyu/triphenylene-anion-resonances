================================================================================
MAD-SIGMA (ROBUST SIGMA): WHAT IT IS AND WHY IT WORKS
Worked through on Automatic-RVP data, A2_root4_fit2
================================================================================

Notation used throughout:
    x            a single value -- here the 'imag' column of
                 clustering_input.csv, i.e. the imaginary part of a complex
                 resonance energy (-Gamma/2), one per Pade window
    median(x)    the middle value of the sorted list
    MAD          Median Absolute Deviation = median( |x - median(x)| )
    MAD-sigma    1.4826 x MAD          (also called "robust sigma")
    std          ordinary standard deviation
    k            the cut threshold, in units of MAD-sigma


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PROBLEM YOU ARE TRYING TO SOLVE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A2_root4_fit2 produces 633 Pade roots in clustering_input.csv; the existing
relative-error filter at src/clustering.py line 30 (|imag_err/imag| < 0.25)
cuts that to 308. Those 308 are the set used everywhere in this document, and
every percentage quoted below is relative to them, not to the raw 633. The MAD
trim described here is a SECOND filter, applied to the 308 survivors.

WHAT THE 308 VALUES ARE:
    Each row of clustering_input.csv is one complex resonance energy, obtained
    by evaluating a Pade approximant at its stationary point over one window of
    the stable zone (src/pade.py lines 70-71):

        real = E_r            the resonance position
        imag = -Gamma/2       the imaginary part of the same complex energy

    src/pade.py line 82 keeps only imag < 0, which is why every value below is
    negative. The 308 are 308 INDEPENDENT ESTIMATES OF THE SAME PHYSICAL
    QUANTITY, each from a different Pade window. That is what makes the outlier
    question meaningful: they should all agree, so the ones that do not are
    numerical failures, not physics.

So: most of the 308 are the real resonance, and a handful are numerical
garbage. You want a rule that says "this point is too far out, drop it."

Any such rule needs exactly two things:

    1. A CENTER  -- too far out FROM WHERE?
    2. A RULER   -- too far COMPARED TO WHAT?

That is all. MAD-sigma is simply a particular choice of those two things.
Everything else follows from that choice.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE OBVIOUS CHOICE, AND WHY IT FAILS *ON CONTAMINATED DATA*
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

NOTE: everything below is conditional on contamination being present. On CLEAN
data the mean and std are not merely acceptable, they are strictly BETTER than
the median and MAD. See section 10(b).

The textbook answer is center = MEAN, ruler = STANDARD DEVIATION.
For the 308 imag values in A2_root4_fit2:

    mean = -1.806e-02        std = 9.470e-02

Now look at where the points actually live:

    the 8 most negative     the middle of the pack      the 5 largest
      -8.149e-01              -5.970e-04                  -3.537e-05
      -6.019e-01              -5.956e-04                  -7.981e-06
      -5.914e-01              -5.908e-04  <-- median      -7.190e-06
      -5.634e-01              -5.892e-04                  -5.419e-06
      -5.154e-01              -5.821e-04                  -4.107e-06
      -5.112e-01              -5.797e-04
      -4.326e-01              -5.794e-04
      -3.574e-01

THE MEAN, -1.806e-02, IS NOT NEAR A SINGLE ONE OF THE 308 POINTS.
It sits in empty space, roughly 30x further out than where the bulk lives.

Why? Because 11 junk points at about -0.5 contribute roughly -5.0 to the sum,
and -5.0 / 308 = -1.6e-02. Those 11 points -- 3.6% of the data -- ARE
essentially the entire mean. The other 297 barely register.

The std is worse still. It is built from SQUARED deviations, so a point that is
1000x too far away contributes 1,000,000x too much.

AND HERE IS THE TRAP:
    You wanted to use the spread to identify the outliers.
    But the outliers are what set the spread.
    The bad points inflate the very ruler you were going to measure them with.

It is circular. You are trying to catch a thief using a witness the thief
bribed.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. WHAT MAD-SIGMA DOES INSTEAD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Swap both choices for RANK-BASED ones -- quantities that depend on where values
sit in the sorted order, not on how big they are.

STEP 1 -- CENTER = THE MEDIAN.

    Sort all 308 values, take the middle one:

        median = -5.900e-04

    That sits right in the thick of the data, where 99% of the points are.
    Compare with the mean at -1.806e-02.

STEP 2 -- MEASURE EVERY POINT'S DISTANCE FROM THAT MEDIAN.

        |x - median|     for all 308 points

STEP 3 -- RULER = THE MEDIAN OF THOSE DISTANCES.

    This is the MAD, the Median Absolute Deviation. Sorting the distances:

        smallest:  8.26e-07  8.26e-07  5.55e-06  6.96e-06  ...
        middle one (#154 of 308):  2.139e-04    <-- this is the MAD
        largest:   ...  5.63e-01  5.91e-01  6.01e-01  8.14e-01

        MAD = 2.139e-04

    LOOK AT WHAT JUST HAPPENED. The four largest deviations are 5.6e-01 to
    8.1e-01 -- three to four orders of magnitude bigger than the MAD. And they
    had ZERO effect on it. The MAD only asks "which distance sits in the middle
    of the sorted list?" Making a big value even bigger does not move it out of
    last place, so nothing changes.

STEP 4 -- THE 1.4826 FACTOR (SEE THE CORRECTION IN SECTION 10).

    The MAD is on a different scale than std. Multiplying by 1.4826 rescales it
    so that "6 sigma" evokes a familiar magnitude:

        MAD-sigma = 1.4826 x 2.139e-04 = 3.171e-04

    This is a NAMING CONVENTION, NOT A COMPUTATION. The factor is the scipy /
    astropy standard (scipy.stats.median_abs_deviation(x, scale='normal')
    returns exactly 1.482602218505602 x MAD), which is why it is worth keeping
    -- it makes the threshold parameter portable and recognizable.

    But it is absorbed entirely into the threshold k, and the procedure is
    INVARIANT to dropping it. Do not read it as a claim about the data.
    Section 10 gives the proof and the correction.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE CORE CONCEPT: BREAKDOWN POINT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This is the one idea worth remembering.

    BREAKDOWN POINT = what fraction of your data has to be garbage before the
                      statistic itself becomes garbage.

    MEAN and STD:     0%.
        One bad point is enough. Push a single value to infinity and the mean
        goes to infinity with it. Zero protection, by construction.

    MEDIAN and MAD:   50%.
        To drag the median off the bulk you would need to add more than 154 bad
        points -- they would have to outnumber the good data. Eleven junk points
        cannot do it. Neither could a hundred.

That is the entire mechanism. MAD-SIGMA GIVES YOU A RULER THE OUTLIERS CANNOT
STRETCH, which breaks the circularity, which is what lets a single pass work
with no iteration.

(This is also why classic sigma-clipping has to loop: it uses std, so pass one's
ruler is corrupt, so you clip, re-measure, and clip again, hoping it converges.
MAD needs no loop -- it is already correct the first time.)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. THE CONSEQUENCE THAT SURPRISES PEOPLE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    6 robust sigma   = 1.903e-03   ->  window  -2.493e-03 .. +1.313e-03
    3 ordinary sigma = 2.841e-01   ->  window  -3.022e-01 .. +2.661e-01

    (actual data range: -8.1492e-01 .. -4.1067e-06)

A 6-SIGMA ROBUST CUT IS 149x TIGHTER THAN A 3-SIGMA ORDINARY CUT.

"Six sigma" sounds permissive and "three sigma" sounds strict, but the sigma is
a different LENGTH in each case. The ordinary 3-sigma window is so wide that it
spans nearly the entire data range including most of the junk -- it flags only
11 points and misses the two subtle ones. The robust 6-sigma window hugs the
actual data and catches all 13.

So k = 6 is not a loose cut. It is a cut that is tight BECAUSE the ruler is
honest.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. HOW IT TIES BACK TO THE PIPELINE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Nothing above changes what gets REPORTED. MAD-sigma is used purely as a
DETECTOR, not as a replacement for any statistic in the output. The flow is:

    1. MAD-sigma identifies the bad rows and drops them. For A2_root4_fit2:
       13 flagged on 'imag', 14 flagged on 'real', 16 in the union (11 rows are
       bad in both). 308 - 16 = 292 rows survive. See section 8.

    2. StandardScaler (src/clustering.py line 33) then computes its ORDINARY
       std on the survivors -- and that std is now 3.28e-04 instead of
       9.47e-02, a 289x correction, because the corruption is gone.

    3. DBSCAN gets a sensibly scaled space, so the eps sweep explores properly
       instead of collapsing after 16 iterations (the premature break at
       src/clustering.py line 119).

    4. The imag_coeff_of_var written to output.dat is still the ordinary
       std/mean it always was -- but computed on a clean cluster, so it finally
       comes in under the 6.5% gate.

MAD-sigma appears once, does one job, and leaves no trace in the reported
physics.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. WHICH COLUMNS ARE FILTERED: BOTH real AND imag
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Sections 1-7 walked through the 'imag' column alone, for clarity. The actual
rule is applied INDEPENDENTLY TO BOTH 'real' AND 'imag', and a row is dropped
if it is an outlier in EITHER one (a union, not an intersection).

That is not belt-and-braces. The two columns fail independently, and which one
fails depends on the dataset. Points flagged at k = 6, broken down by which
column caught them:

    dataset            n | real only  imag only  both |  union
    -----------------------------------------------------------
    A2_root1_fit1    421 |         0          7     1 |      8
    A2_root2_fit1    451 |         0          9     0 |      9
    A2_root2_fit2    367 |        84          1    11 |     96
    A2_root3_fit1    292 |        27          6     3 |     36
    A2_root3_fit2    300 |         1          7     9 |     17
    A2_root4_fit1    569 |         0         70     0 |     70
    A2_root4_fit2    308 |         3          2    11 |     16

    A2_root2_fit2 WOULD BE ALMOST ENTIRELY MISSED by an imag-only rule: 84 of
    its 96 bad points are outliers in energy POSITION while their widths look
    perfectly reasonable. Same story in A2_root3_fit1 (27 of 36).

    A2_root4_fit1 is the mirror image: all 70 are width-only, position clean.

    Note the 'both' column is small everywhere. Most junk Pade roots are wrong
    in exactly ONE coordinate, which is precisely why the union is required.

THE std/MAD-sigma RATIO IS A FREE CONTAMINATION METER
-----------------------------------------------------

Comparing each column's ordinary std against its MAD-sigma tells you, before
dropping anything, whether that column is contaminated at all. A ratio near 1
means the two estimators agree, so nothing is skewing the std.

    A2_root3_fit1    real  1.2989e-03   5.1204e-02         39.4
    A2_root3_fit1    imag  6.6532e-04   8.1170e-02        122.0

    thousand times larger.

THE RULE IS SELF-LIMITING
-------------------------

    The two columns with a ratio BELOW 1 -- A2_root1_fit1 'real' and
    A2_root4_fit1 'real', where MAD-sigma is actually LARGER than std -- flag
    exactly ZERO real-only points (see the table above). Those columns have a
    broad but genuine spread, and the trim correctly leaves them alone.

    The filter only cuts where there is something to cut. It does not eat into
    healthy columns.

WHY NOT alpha AND theta
-----------------------

    (a) DBSCAN never clusters on them. src/clustering.py line 80 fits on
        scaled_data[['real','imag']] only, so the scale of alpha and theta
        cannot affect the epsilon sweep.

    (b) theta is an ANGLE. A median absolute deviation is meaningless if the
        values ever straddle the +/-pi branch cut.

    alpha and theta are carried through unchanged and reported for whichever
    rows survive the real/imag cut.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. THE RULE IN CODE FORM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

    drop = pd.Index([])
    for col in ['real', 'imag']:                     # BOTH columns
        med = np.median(c[col].values)
        mad_sigma = 1.4826 * np.median(np.abs(c[col].values - med))
        if mad_sigma <= 0:                           # degenerate-data guard
            continue
        drop = drop.union(c.index[np.abs(c[col] - med) > k * mad_sigma])
    cleaned = c.drop(drop)

    with k = 6.

Properties of this rule:
    - applied to 'real' and 'imag' only
    - UNION across the two columns (drop if bad in either)
    - single pass, no iteration (see section 4: the MAD is not corrupted, so
      there is nothing to converge toward)
    - guard skips a column whose MAD-sigma is zero, which happens if more than
      half the rows share an identical value
    - inserted between src/clustering.py line 30 (the existing imag_err
      filter) and line 33 (StandardScaler)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. CORRECTION: TWO THINGS SECTIONS 2 AND 3 STATED MISLEADINGLY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

MISLEADING CLAIM 1: that the mean is simply a bad centre.
MISLEADING CLAIM 2: that the 1.4826 factor "makes MAD-sigma agree with std",
                    which implies the data is Gaussian and that the factor does
                    real work. Neither is true.

(a) THE DATA IS NOT GAUSSIAN.

    Shapiro-Wilk on the k=6 core rejects normality in every column of every
    dataset, p from 8e-07 down to 5e-24. Skew runs -1.77 to +0.87, excess
    kurtosis -1.09 to +3.44.

    The shape is a hard-walled, one-sided, skewed distribution. The upper wall
    is PHYSICAL, not statistical: src/pade.py line 82 enforces imag < 0, and
    Gamma > 0. The wall position predicts the observed cutoff exactly:

        dataset          imag=0 in MAD-sigma    last populated bin
        A2_root1_fit1          +1.26            [+0.5, +1.5)
        A2_root2_fit1          +1.09            [+0.5, +1.5)
        A2_root3_fit1          +1.94            [+1.5, +2.5)
        A2_root3_fit2          +1.08            [+0.5, +1.5)
        A2_root4_fit1          +2.50            [+1.5, +2.5)
        A2_root4_fit2          +1.94            [+1.5, +2.5)

(b) ON CLEAN GAUSSIAN DATA THE MEAN AND STD WOULD BE *BETTER*, NOT EQUAL.

    Monte Carlo, n=300, 4000 trials, RMSE of the estimate:

        contamination          mean    median  |     std   MAD-sigma
        none (pure Gaussian)  0.058     0.073  |   0.041       0.067   <- mean/std win
        0.5% junk             4.754     0.073  |  80.38        0.068
        4% junk (this data)  11.435     0.073  | 198.37        0.084

    The median is ~1.3x WORSE than the mean on clean Gaussian data, and
    MAD-sigma ~1.6x worse than std. Those are real, known efficiency costs.

    THE JUSTIFICATION IS CONTAMINATION, NOT SHAPE. The crossover happens below
    0.5% junk, where the median already wins by 65x and MAD-sigma by 1184x. At
    the ~4% contamination in this data it is 156x and 2370x. Sections 2 and 4
    are correct about the mechanism; they simply omitted that the advantage is
    conditional on contamination being present.

(c) THE 1.4826 IS INERT. IT IS A REPARAMETERIZATION, NOT A COMPUTATION.

    The threshold is k * 1.4826 * MAD. Only the PRODUCT reaches the comparison,
    so the constant folds into k:

        k = 6      WITH the constant     ==     k = 8.8956  WITHOUT it

    Verified: both drop the identical rows in all seven datasets.

    The constant would be load-bearing in exactly two situations, and NEITHER
    applies here:

        1. If k were derived from Gaussian tail probability ("reject at 1 in
           1e9, therefore k=6"). It was not -- k=6 was chosen EMPIRICALLY by
           sweeping k and observing which value rescued A2_root3_fit2.

        2. If MAD-sigma were reported as an uncertainty. It is not -- it never
           leaves the detection step. The imag_coeff_of_var in output.dat is an
           ordinary std/mean computed downstream on the surviving cluster.

    Because both are false, non-normality cannot corrupt anything. A wrong
    calibration constant is mathematically identical to a different k, and the
    results were already shown stable across k = 6 to 20, a 3.3x range.

    The constant is kept only because it is the scipy/astropy convention, which
    makes k portable and recognizable. That is a legibility argument, not a
    statistical one.

(d) THE TRIM ELIMINATES ITS OWN JUSTIFICATION, WHICH IS THE RIGHT BEHAVIOUR.

    After the k=6 trim, mean and median agree to 0.02-0.32 std and std/MAD-sigma
    falls to 0.7-2.1 in six of seven datasets. The choice of centre stops
    mattering once the contamination is gone -- exactly as the Gaussian
    intuition predicts.

    EXCEPTION: A2_root2_fit2 still shows std/MAD-sigma = 92 (real) and 379
    (imag) AFTER trimming. Its core is not "good population plus outliers" but
    genuinely multi-scale. This is the explanation for the anomaly noted
    elsewhere: root2_fit2 is the one dataset whose CoV DEGRADES at k=6
    (3.96% -> 6.42%). Treat its result with more suspicion than the others.

(e) SAFETY CHECK.

    Every cluster the pipeline reports on untrimmed data falls INSIDE the k=6
    window, in all four currently-working runs. The trim does not cut physics.
